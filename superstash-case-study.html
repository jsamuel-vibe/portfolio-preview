Superstash: from collecting inspiration to using what I know

I designed and built Superstash to bring references and personal notes into one place. The longer-term ambition is to make that collection useful context for AI-assisted design.

Personal project · Solo design and development · Working product, used personally

The starting problem

The collection kept growing. Its usefulness didn’t.

My design work and learning happened across the same collection of tools. I saved interface references, screenshots, articles, writing inspiration and ideas for things I wanted to build.

Some went into Pinterest. Others ended up in Notion, downloads, screenshots or chat apps. My own thoughts were scattered too, often separated from the reference that prompted them.

When I needed something for a project, I had to remember both what I had saved and where I had put it. Sometimes I returned to studying something I had already encountered because I couldn’t bring the earlier material back into my work. I wanted a way to make that collection useful again.

Who I designed for

A designer collecting references and recording his own thinking.

I developed a working persona, Joselu, based mainly on my own experience and supported by exploratory research. He moves between professional design work, learning and personal projects.

He collects visual references, but also saves critiques, writing, app ideas and notes about what he is learning. His problem is not a lack of material. It is the effort of maintaining that material and finding the relevant part when he needs it.

That profile brought two needs together: collecting references and recording personal thinking. It was a starting point for the design, not an interview-validated picture of a wider audience.

The larger ambition

Giving AI useful context from what I collect, learn and think.

A collection of references shows what catches my attention. Notes and critiques add something else: what I noticed, questioned or wanted to try.

Connecting that material to an AI tool could help resurface forgotten references, bring earlier thoughts into a current project and inform new iterations. It could also help me apply what I had learned rather than leave it sitting in another note.

That was the direction, not a capability I had already delivered. The external API connection is still planned.

Choosing the first release

A foundation I could realistically build and maintain alone.

I was responsible for the design and development, with limited time to explore, build, fix bugs and refine the interface.

I started with browser capture, a shared library for references and notes, and AI-generated descriptions and tags. The collection supports different kinds of material, including images, web pages, articles, snippets, posts and notes.

The external AI connection remained outside the first release. Before expanding the product, I needed the existing experience to become more reliable and useful. This meant making scope decisions alongside design decisions.

These drafts are the alternatives I considered, rather than a sequence of tested improvements.

A smaller first release, built around quick capture

Quick note-taking became a priority through using Superstash myself. I brought a note field directly into the gallery, keeping it immediately available without letting it dominate the collection.

I chose a compact icon rail instead of an expanded sidebar, and kept the product centred on the main workspace and settings. This was a simpler version to build, with fewer screens to design and maintain.

I preferred the visual polish of some earlier explorations. For this release, I chose to get a useful version working sooner and leave further refinement for later.

Learning through use

Easy saving created a different problem.

My first browser-saving approach removed almost every step. A Save button appeared when I hovered over an image, making capture a one-click action.

Through using it, I found myself saving too indiscriminately. The library started becoming another pile of material I might never return to. Effortless capture could help me collect more without helping me build a more useful collection.

I explored a right-click saving flow that would make saving a more deliberate choice. It also introduced trade-offs around discoverability and how the interaction would work on a phone. The right-click interaction is an implemented iteration; I have not yet validated whether it leads to a more useful collection.

AI in the working product

Giving saved material context.

AI-generated descriptions and tags are part of the working product. I wrote the prompts to describe each save in language that would be useful to a person returning to it.

This adds context without requiring me to name and categorise every item manually. The library also has search and content-type filters for returning to saved material.

Automatically describing a save is different from understanding which reference would help with a particular project. Connecting the collection to an external AI tool remains the longer-term goal.

What changed in my approach

Using the product changed how I judged the work.

The home screen needed to support what I actually did, which made quick notes more important than they had been in the initial explorations.

The saving interaction showed me that removing friction from one step could create work elsewhere. Working alone also made prioritisation unavoidable: I had to distinguish the product’s longer-term ambition from the version I could realistically build and maintain.

These are observations from personal use, not evidence of outcomes for a wider audience.

Where it goes next

Refine the working experience before expanding the AI capabilities.

My immediate priorities are fixing bugs and refining the existing UI and workflows.

The longer-term goal remains connecting Superstash to AI tools so that collected references and personal notes can inform new work.

That direction will need testing beyond my own use. The important question is whether Superstash helps people return to useful knowledge and apply it, not simply collect more of it.
